# City Watch: The Lantern Files — 32 additional case briefs

**Status: design packets only.** CW-NC-17 to CW-NC-48 extend the [original 16 briefs](City-Watch-16-Case-Deployment-Briefs.md). None are registered, implemented, balanced, or deployed. Routes are candidate sequences between existing locations; they do not certify adjacency, an entrance, a particular standing point, or a new map link. Use existing character contacts where verified and unnamed residents for new speaking roles. No new canon claims.

**Implementation fit:** **Enquiry** means the existing clue, finding and filing flow can carry the core case. **Extension** means the richer choice requires a real additional action or persisted scene state. These are design estimates. Some dialogue choices in an enquiry should persist in the case instance; no citywide consequences or persistent world edits are implied.

## CW-NC-17 — A Cart for the Wrong Funeral · enquiry
**Route:** Colon at Watch Square → public Small Gods approach → Market carrier’s stop. Nobby can read a smudged cart mark.
**Evidence:** Two families booked the same cart at different times. The carrier has a legitimate receipt for each; only one has a separately witnessed alteration. Someone changed the earlier booking to free the cart for a paying customer.
**Choice and filing:** Get transport arranged first, or trace the altered receipt while the same clerk is still present. File the booking fraud only if the alteration can be tied to its author.
**Acceptance:** Both families get a clear outcome. No cemetery or temple interior is needed.

## CW-NC-18 — The Door That Was Knocked Twice · extension
**Route:** A public Shades frontage → neighbouring public crossroads → Watch Square. Nobby can watch one doorway while the player waits at the other.
**Evidence:** Residents think a double knock marks houses for burglary. A laundress uses it to warn customers that their washing is about to get wet; a separate visitor is using the commotion to steal from unattended steps.
**Choice and filing:** Warn the street and stop the next theft, or watch one collection to identify the opportunist. Keep the laundress out of the allegation unless there is actual evidence against her.
**Acceptance:** The two contacts remain distinguishable in the Notebook; a missed stakeout has another window.

## CW-NC-19 — The Brass Weight · enquiry
**Route:** Market stalls → another existing public stall → Carrot at Watch Square.
**Evidence:** A seller’s scale underweighs produce. Compare the disputed weight with a control and inspect the stamp: the stallholder borrowed it from a neighbour who may have been sold the same faulty batch.
**Choice and filing:** Arrange refunds immediately or preserve the weight and trace its supplier. Underweight goods can be proved without proving the seller knew.
**Acceptance:** The case uses evidence text, not changes to shop prices, inventory or save balances.

## CW-NC-20 — Whose Round Is It? · enquiry
**Route:** Watch Square → Mended Drum public entrance → Market. Colon checks the Watch’s records; Nobby knows the regulars.
**Evidence:** A tavern keeper thinks a Watch officer put drinks on a patrol account. The time in the ledger is real, but the tab was opened by a courier carrying a coat with a borrowed badge.
**Choice and filing:** Help the keeper correct the bill first or follow the borrowed-badge lead. The badge is a lead; seeing the courier claim Watch authority is the evidence of impersonation.
**Acceptance:** The existing Mended Drum brawl still starts only from its own prompt.

## CW-NC-21 — The Window That Wasn’t Broken · enquiry
**Route:** University public approach → public workshop frontage → Watch Square. An existing University contact explains the report.
**Evidence:** A crate is missing and glass lies outside, but all the panes are intact. The shard fits a broken display placed outside later. A dispatch entry shows when the crate left.
**Choice and filing:** Clear the alleged break-in or continue with the missing-crate inquiry. The dispatch entry proves removal, not who carried it.
**Acceptance:** Inspecting the window and questioning the workshop contact in either order yields the same available findings; no building state changes.

## CW-NC-22 — The Cost of a Dry Crossing · extension
**Route:** An approved bridge approach → public quay steps or nearby dry walking point → Carrot. Reuse an existing accessible approach.
**Evidence:** Someone charges a bogus “flood fee.” The temporary warning looks legitimate, but upstream and downstream accounts disagree about when the alleged closure started. A witnessed payment identifies the collector.
**Choice and filing:** Escort a waiting group through the lawful route or stay to catch the collector taking money. The public can be helped without proving fraud.
**Acceptance:** Navigation never sends the player onto water, a held link, or a cart-only lane. No bridge closure is applied.

## CW-NC-23 — Blue Chalk, White Chalk · enquiry
**Route:** Market seller → public workshop frontage → Colon. Dibbler offers a street-level observation, not the answer.
**Evidence:** Chalk on crates attracts accusations of theft. One mark means a crate is due back to its owner; a second mark was added to redirect it. A retained tally connects only the second mark to the diversion.
**Choice and filing:** Return the crates you can identify or follow the tally to find who was redirecting them. Unidentified crates go in the unresolved report.
**Acceptance:** Both marks have text descriptions; colour recognition is never necessary.

## CW-NC-24 — The Unsent Letter · enquiry
**Route:** Post Office exterior → an existing public Market contact → Watch Square. No overlap with Postage Due dispatch logic.
**Evidence:** A threatening letter is blamed on the person whose name appears at the bottom. The recipient received a hand-delivered copy; a witness saw the named writer refuse to finish the original.
**Choice and filing:** Warn the intended target or establish who copied the unfinished letter. Separate the authenticity of the signature from proof of delivery.
**Acceptance:** No private post is opened and no existing parcel or postal-case state changes.

## CW-NC-25 — One Boot at the Quay · extension
**Route:** Quayside → an existing cobbler or public workshop frontage → a legal dry dock approach. Nobby accompanies; Colon debriefs.
**Evidence:** A boot near the river starts a drowning rumour. The cobbler recognises it as a spare waiting for repair. The boot was planted to distract from someone waiting to leave under pressure.
**Choice and filing:** Escort the person to a safe public spot when found, or keep the escape route under observation. Confirm their safety independently of the accusation of coercion.
**Acceptance:** No required step is on water. An interrupted escort can resume from the last legal checkpoint.

## CW-NC-26 — The Last Stool · enquiry
**Route:** Mended Drum public frontage → Market furniture or workshop frontage → Carrot.
**Evidence:** A tavern keeper reports a stolen stool. The person seen carrying it has a repair ticket, but for a different stool. Matching the carved underside and reading the dated order reveal two separate pickups.
**Choice and filing:** Return the stool without alleging intent or follow who arranged the second pickup. The witness only saw the legitimate carrier.
**Acceptance:** The brawl prompt and venue entry remain available as before; no object is removed from the permanent scene.

## CW-NC-27 — The Empty Evidence Bag · enquiry
**Route:** Colon at Watch Square → public Watch Square contact → existing Market buyer.
**Evidence:** A complainant says the Watch lost an item after taking custody. The bag number exists, but the street receipt and the desk record use different bag numbers. The trade witness confirms the item had already been sold before either receipt.
**Choice and filing:** Correct the custody record first or establish who deliberately used the mix-up. Never turn an administrative mistake into evidence of theft by an officer.
**Acceptance:** Uses case-specific records only; no existing custody, inventory or arrest record changes.

## CW-NC-28 — No Change Given · enquiry
**Route:** Dibbler’s active pitch → another public Market stop → Watch Square.
**Evidence:** Customers complain that change is continually one coin short. Compare a clean purchase and a witnessed two-customer exchange: a runner is intercepting change meant for the next buyer.
**Choice and filing:** Recover a customer’s loss during the exchange, or hold back to demonstrate the pattern. One short sale alone supports repayment, not the full allegation.
**Acceptance:** Test purchases are a case interaction and never alter ordinary vendor stock or charge the player.

## CW-NC-29 — The Watchman’s Shadow · extension
**Route:** Watch Square → public Palace approach → adjacent lawful public observation point. Carrot checks official patrol movements; Nobby follows at a distance.
**Evidence:** A uniformed figure appears immediately before a string of shop closures. The figure is a hired messenger in a borrowed coat; the person issuing the false closure orders is elsewhere.
**Choice and filing:** Stop the impersonation now or have Nobby follow the written order back. The messenger can cooperate without automatically becoming the guilty party.
**Acceptance:** All observation and handover remain outside restricted Palace space; ending the trail preserves the correct partial finding.

## CW-NC-30 — The Loan of a Ledger · enquiry
**Route:** Royal Bank public frontage → public Market contact → Colon. No vault, account or indoor access is required.
**Evidence:** A trader claims the bank seized a book. An acknowledgement shows it was loaned for a single query and returned. A second trader received a facsimile with an added debt entry.
**Choice and filing:** Show the false entry to the debtor first or trace the copying job. The return receipt clears the bank; the comparison plus a witness support forgery.
**Acceptance:** Distinct from the missing-ledger campaign case; it neither reads nor writes that case’s flags.

## CW-NC-31 — A Witness for Hire · enquiry
**Route:** Watch Square → Market → public University approach. Nobby can point out repeated wording across accounts.
**Evidence:** Several people claim to have seen the same theft from the same spot. One describes a detail that cannot be seen from that frontage. Another names who paid them to learn the account.
**Choice and filing:** Discard the rehearsed statements and re-investigate, or take a corroborated soliciting-witness case back to Colon. Failed testimony does not prove the original incident was staged.
**Acceptance:** The impossible sightline is explained by a readable observation prompt, never by demanding precise camera placement.

## CW-NC-32 — The Quiet Delivery · extension
**Route:** Docks → one existing public bridge crossing → a quiet public market frontage. Carrot briefs; use the existing partner if appropriate.
**Evidence:** Someone flags a sealed delivery as stolen. The receiver matches the seal and tally, but a person overhead has been trying to force a different drop. The attempted redirection is the contested act.
**Choice and filing:** Walk the courier safely to the genuine receiver or let the partner observe who asks for the diverted delivery. The courier can go free while the diversion remains unproved.
**Acceptance:** Cancel and resume without losing the courier’s last legal position or attaching them to an unrelated route.

## CW-NC-33 — The Borrowed Voice · enquiry
**Route:** University public approach → public market frontage → Watch Square. Use an existing faculty contact if verified.
**Evidence:** A claimant insists they received a spoken order from a wizard. Two witnesses remember identical words, including an unlikely mistake. A written notice at the market shows the lines were being repeated from a copied script.
**Choice and filing:** Clear the supposed speaker and warn the intended victim, or trace who supplied the script. Matching words link the accounts, not the identity of the reader.
**Acceptance:** All spoken clues are available as text. No spell, voice asset, or new canonical power is needed.

## CW-NC-34 — The Newspaper on the Wrong Step · enquiry
**Route:** Existing newspaper seller → two lawful public frontages on the same route → Carrot.
**Evidence:** A householder believes someone is marking vacant premises with newspapers. The delivery round explains one set of papers; a second set contains folded notes with viewing times for paid lookouts.
**Choice and filing:** Remove the signals to deter theft or identify who replaces one when it is moved. Separate an innocent delivery round from the organised signals.
**Acceptance:** Existing Times/Inquirer stock, newspaper ownership, and home entrances stay unchanged.

## CW-NC-35 — One Place Left · extension
**Route:** Existing lodging service → Mended Drum approach → Watch Square. Mrs Palm can help establish when a booking was made.
**Evidence:** Two guests arrive with the same confirmed bed. The keeper accepted one valid booking; a reseller copied the receipt to sell the second. A numbered note and a witnessed payment identify the reseller.
**Choice and filing:** Secure a lawful room for the stranded guest immediately or ask them to help arrange a witnessed refund. Give them a way to decline without blocking the report.
**Acceptance:** No lodging stock, check-in, or save effects unless the player separately uses the existing lodging service.

## CW-NC-36 — The Empty Stool at Closing · enquiry
**Route:** Existing public Market pitch → a lawful shop approach → Carrot at Watch Square.
**Evidence:** A trader’s pitch is empty at closing, yet takings were recorded after they left. A neighbour used the space with permission; someone third-party carried off the cash box during the handover.
**Choice and filing:** Reconcile the accounts to clear the neighbour or pursue the person seen carrying the box. The ordinary handover cannot be filed as theft on its own.
**Acceptance:** The case remains distinct from CW-NC-26; it is a Market handover case, and no tavern interaction is used.

## CW-NC-37 — A Matter of Inches · enquiry
**Route:** Shades street frontage → existing workshop threshold → Carrot.
**Evidence:** A shop disputes a charge for obstructing the street. The inspection was made from a chalk mark that was moved overnight. Compare the original witness mark with a delivery footprint to see who gained space from the shift.
**Choice and filing:** Resolve the immediate obstruction complaint or investigate the false measurement. An honest dispute is a valid outcome if motive remains unknown.
**Acceptance:** No pavement widths, collision, plot boundaries or legal routes change.

## CW-NC-38 — The Half-Penny Chorus · extension
**Route:** Opera House public exterior → public Market approach → Watch Square. Nobby can observe while the player speaks to a complainant.
**Evidence:** A chorus of identical heckles distracts a queue while fake refund tickets circulate. One heckler is only working for a promised wage; the ticket seller is elsewhere.
**Choice and filing:** Stop the fake refunds immediately or watch who pays the hecklers. Delaying costs time, but lack of a complete case never erases people you protected.
**Acceptance:** No backstage access or audio dependency; the existing Opera encounter stays available on its own trigger.

## CW-NC-39 — The Name Under the Paint · enquiry
**Route:** Docks → public workshop frontage → Carrot. An unnamed worker helps compare the crate markings.
**Evidence:** A cargo owner identifies their goods by a painted name. A faint older mark and the weight receipt show the crate came from another delivery. A worker can confirm who asked for fresh paint.
**Choice and filing:** Return what can be proved to belong to the true owner or hold it while the repaint order is traced. The crate’s contents and the claimant’s intentions are separate findings.
**Acceptance:** No requirement to open an interior, carry a heavy prop, or recolour permanent art.

## CW-NC-40 — A Debt to Nobody · enquiry
**Route:** Mrs Palm’s existing contact → Royal Bank outdoor meeting point → Colon at Watch Square.
**Evidence:** Several people receive demands for a debt owed to a person nobody can find. The original agreements list a legitimate payee, but the demands give a new collection point that payee never approved.
**Choice and filing:** Tell debtors where to verify the obligation or find the false collector. Preventing payment does not cancel a genuine debt.
**Acceptance:** No banking transactions or player money changes occur in the case itself.

## CW-NC-41 — Keep the Lantern Lit · extension
**Route:** Two lawful night-time street points in Watch Square or the Shades → Carrot. Partner can remain with the person being followed.
**Evidence:** A worker says someone follows them home only when the street is busy. The unwanted follower starts at a public meeting point, but a separate person is passing them route information.
**Choice and filing:** Stay visible and accompany the worker to safety or arrange a consensual observation of the handoff. The worker can opt out of the stakeout without losing help.
**Acceptance:** A daylight start offers a later observation window. The player can finish the safety outcome without catching anyone.

## CW-NC-42 — Tickets for Tomorrow · enquiry
**Route:** Opera House public exterior → existing Market seller or contact → Colon.
**Evidence:** Duplicate tickets have apparently valid serial numbers. An authentic stub shows the same number series was used for a different day. A buyer saw someone trimming the printed date off an old batch.
**Choice and filing:** Arrange a correction for ticket holders or trace the seller before alerting the queue. Two tickets with the same number are insufficient without the date and seller evidence.
**Acceptance:** No new ticket purchase system, timed show, or Opera interior is necessary.

## CW-NC-43 — The Sack With Two Tags · enquiry
**Route:** Post Office public approach → quayside handover point → Carrot.
**Evidence:** A sack’s two tags describe different destinations. One is an original routing tag, the other an old return label. A matching dispatch mark establishes which tag was applied last; the route witness identifies who switched it.
**Choice and filing:** Return the sack unopened or keep it at a witnessed handover while the record is checked. File the routing error without accusing anyone if the swap cannot be tied to them.
**Acceptance:** No changes to Postage Due, parcel ownership, or the live delivery ledger.

## CW-NC-44 — Tally at Low Water · enquiry
**Route:** High, dry public quayside → existing bridge approach → Watch Square. Nobby helps compare the two counts.
**Evidence:** A watchman’s tally says a crate never crossed. The cargo was split into two hand loads, each entered under a different description. A witness explains why only one looked like the original goods.
**Choice and filing:** Clear the carrier and repair the count, or trace the person who deliberately filed a false shortfall. The count alone supports no arrest.
**Acceptance:** Every evidence point is above water and on an existing public route; no sidestep across a bank edge.

## CW-NC-45 — The House With No Complaints · enquiry
**Route:** Existing Shades tenement exterior → public street contact → Watch Square. Carrot asks for the residents’ own account.
**Evidence:** A claims agent presents letters saying residents are satisfied with promised repairs. The wording is copied, but one person genuinely signed a receipt for materials rather than a statement that work was finished.
**Choice and filing:** Help residents record actual repairs needed or trace who filed the false statements. Do not treat silence, absence, or reluctance to sign as evidence that residents agree.
**Acceptance:** The case is solvable outdoors; a reluctant resident has a written or independent evidence substitute.

## CW-NC-46 — A Coat for the Captain · extension
**Route:** Public Watch Square → existing tailor or workshop frontage → Market. Carrot confirms he placed no order.
**Evidence:** A commission made in Carrot’s name asks for a watch-style coat. The maker can establish that it is ordinary work; a public witness can later see whether it is used to collect a payment under false authority.
**Choice and filing:** Tell the maker what you know, or arrange for Carrot to watch the handover. A coat alone is not a crime; report what its wearer actually did.
**Acceptance:** No new equipment, usable uniform, or disguise mechanic. The innocent commission outcome does not seize the item.

## CW-NC-47 — The Street That Was Sold · enquiry
**Route:** Existing Market approach → Watch Square duty desk → the original lawful pitch frontage. Dibbler can be a complainant when present.
**Evidence:** Someone is selling rights to stand at a public pitch. The document looks formal, but is a genuine unrelated receipt with a private note added. A second buyer can identify who took payment.
**Choice and filing:** Warn traders and keep access clear or get payment evidence first. Keep the pitch open under its existing rules in either outcome.
**Acceptance:** No new city law, map authority, fee or interactive shop is introduced.

## CW-NC-48 — The Second Report · extension
**Route:** Colon at Watch Square → one previously visited lawful Market or dock contact → Carrot. A new review of an old incident, not a rewrite of a completed case.
**Evidence:** Someone has copied a filed report and added a paragraph naming a person the Watch never accused. The public copy and the original disagree on the last finding. A witness can say where the altered copy came from.
**Choice and filing:** Publish a correction through an existing case-local public notice or trace the source before correcting it. Clear the person named even if you cannot prove who altered the report.
**Acceptance:** The original saved report stays intact; the correction is a separate case entry and cannot award the original case reward again.
