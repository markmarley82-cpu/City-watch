# City Watch — A second shift with every partner

Ten follow-up briefs for the [first ten partner stories](City-Watch-Partner-Story-Missions.md). An earlier report changes how a person responds, where a lead starts, or what proof the player still needs. Finish either legal outcome of the first story to qualify. A narrow or unresolved report can open a different lead; it must not cut off the officer's story. No cases are implemented or released.

## When Duty offers a follow-up

Offer it on a later patrol with the same eligible officer, after the player files the first story. Use the filed outcome and its actual evidence, not a relationship meter. The player can decline, do an ordinary shift, or select another partner between missions. Don't require a perfect first report and don't rewrite an earlier verdict. If the player filed the first case in an older save, use a neutral opening when the necessary detail isn't present.

Once accepted, preserve the officer, case branch and any encounter through save/resume. The named approaches are candidate anchors to resolve against the live map: no new link, interior or terrain change. The partner's suggestion is a way into the problem, not the right answer.

## CW-PS-11 — Nobby: Change for a Silver Piece
**Previous:** CW-PS-01, The Honest Lookout. **Route:** Market public frontage → Watch Square food pitch → Colon.

A trader claims someone is passing bad silver. Nobby can tell that the coins are fine: the customer was short-changed before the silver ever came out. The trick relies on customers having to answer two questions at once.

**What carries over:** If the first case cleared the child publicly, they come forward with the exact patter they heard. If the player kept watching to identify the payer, a customer instead points out the same lookout working the queue. Neither opening proves who kept the money.

**What you do together:** Ask Nobby to make an ordinary purchase while you watch the change. Then choose between refunding the waiting customers immediately or asking one willing buyer to stay long enough to identify where the missing change goes. Nobby offers to show the trick in debrief; the player has to name the step that made it work.

**Release check:** The demonstration never charges the player or changes real vendor prices; both openings support a proven or narrow report.

## CW-PS-12 — Colon: The Queue Nobody Owned
**Previous:** CW-PS-02, A Lane for Two. **Route:** Opera exterior → public junction → Watch Square.

Two suppliers turn up at once and each insists the other was told to wait. The street is blocking up again. Colon wants to re-use the old waiting lane, but on this shift that would leave an unrelated doorway boxed in.

**What carries over:** If you dispersed the earlier crowd, the porter brings people to the Watch before things escalate. If you held a safe line and traced the first load, the first supplier brings the disputed delivery slip instead.

**What you do together:** Ask Colon to keep one lawful passage clear. Choose whether you stay at the bottleneck or go to compare the suppliers' copies. The one you don't visit first is still reachable through a later witness. Colon backs a safe ending even if the conflicting instructions remain unexplained.

**Release check:** Case flags represent congestion and relief; no real road, door, or Opera approach becomes obstructed.

## CW-PS-13 — Detritus: The Crate Everyone Heard
**Previous:** CW-PS-03, Behind the Barrow. **Route:** Dry quay → public workshop frontage → Watch Square.

A crate falls and bystanders swear they heard someone throw it. Detritus notes that the sound came after the crowd had already turned. The sound was the load shifting inside the crate. Someone packed the weight off-centre to disguise a missing compartment.

**What carries over:** If the earlier report prioritised the injured witness, a worker trusts the patrol enough to demonstrate how the load was carried. If it preserved the previous load evidence, the dispatcher starts by supplying a second copy of the tally.

**What you do together:** Ask Detritus to steady the crate while a consenting worker demonstrates its weight shift from a safe position. Compare the signed tally or clear the blamed carrier first. The demonstration clears the carrier from the throwing allegation; proving who packed the crate needs another account.

**Release check:** Use a fixed case interaction, no simulation of falling loads and no weapon action. Readable text conveys the sound clue.

## CW-PS-14 — Visit: Two Minutes After Closing
**Previous:** CW-PS-04, The Third Account. **Route:** Small Gods public approach → Market pitches → Watch Square.

A tradesperson keeps reopening a resolved dispute because the written settlement leaves out the two minutes they spend closing the stall. Someone has been treating the brief gap as permission to collect a second fee.

**What carries over:** A private first report earns a quiet early meeting. A consented public explanation earns two witnesses willing to meet together, but they remember events in a different order.

**What you do together:** Let Visit ask each person to narrate the sequence without correcting the other. You compare their accounts with who actually collected money. Solve the timing problem, then choose whether to offer an immediate refund arrangement or preserve the payment testimony for filing.

**Release check:** The case accepts either interview order and preserves consent. No actual timeslot, fee or shop balance changes.

## CW-PS-15 — Reg Shoe: The Complaint That Came Back
**Previous:** CW-PS-05, Whose Names Were on the List? **Route:** Shades frontage → Market public contact → Colon.

Residents start receiving their own complaint forms back with extra words written across them. Someone is not suppressing objections this time: they are making the objections sound absurd so nobody will act on them.

**What carries over:** A public correction from the first story means a resident kept a duplicate and trusts the Watch with it. If you preserved the old list for the investigation, a clerk recognises the way a blank line was used and tells you who handled intake.

**What you do together:** Ask Reg to organise a short, orderly hearing, or take statements one at a time. Compare the duplicate with the returned form. File the real complaint promptly, or trace who changed it; never require residents to expose their identities in public to get a correction.

**Release check:** The Notebook keeps the genuine complaint separate from the allegation of tampering. A harmed resident can decline a hearing without ending the case.

## CW-PS-16 — Cheery: A Seal With Nothing to Say
**Previous:** CW-PS-06, What the Wax Kept. **Route:** Post Office exterior → workshop frontage → Carrot.

Someone uses an intact seal as proof that the declared goods are still in a packet. Cheery points out that a seal can establish when a packet was shut; it can't tell you what went in before that.

**What carries over:** If the player released the first parcel after clearing the carrier, the consignee brings their own packing copy. If the player retained a witnessed record, the workshop offers a comparison impression first.

**What you do together:** Ask Cheery to compare the impression and packing record as separate findings. Then choose whether to protect the intended recipient by securing a witnessed return or keep following who certified the contents. The 'intact seal' observation is true on both routes and insufficient for a verdict on its own.

**Release check:** Inspecting the seal cannot mark contents verified. Use no new postal flags or item shipment changes.

## CW-PS-17 — Dorfl: A Word Given in the Street
**Previous:** CW-PS-07, The Promise at the Door. **Route:** Lodging frontage → Market public junction → Watch Square.

Someone quotes a public promise that an item would be held until evening, then claims that the promise made the Watch responsible for replacing it. Dorfl remembers hearing the promise too. It was made by the keeper to the owner, and applied to a different item.

**What carries over:** If you ended the earlier obstruction with a narrow report, an uninvolved lodger confirms the exact words. If you checked the property receipt before filing, the keeper brings the receipt copy first.

**What you do together:** Ask Dorfl to repeat only what he personally heard while the player verifies which item both parties meant. Offer a safe return arrangement or investigate the false claim separately. Both outcomes free the public passage without compelling an agreement.

**Release check:** The displayed text clearly separates speaker, promise, item and source; Dorfl's memory is corroboration, not the entire proof.

## CW-PS-18 — Angua: The Exit Left Open
**Previous:** CW-PS-08, The Runner Who Turned Back. **Route:** Shades public junction → existing adjoining street → Watch Square.

A worker carrying the evening takings keeps leaving by the same exit, even though they know someone is waiting. They are trying to keep a second, bulkier delivery unnoticed on a lawful parallel route. Following the obvious runner leaves the actual transfer unwatched.

**What carries over:** If the earlier satchel was returned quietly, the runner warns you in confidence about the wrong exit. If coercion was reported, a corroborating witness provides the lawful handover point. The worker's trust doesn't depend on a successful chase.

**What you do together:** Put Angua at the known exit while you speak to the person on the other route, or have her intercept only after you corroborate which load belongs to whom. A failed interception turns into an interview and a valid narrow report. This time restraint means knowing which person not to chase.

**Release check:** Both success and failure preserve one saved encounter identity. No detection power, route through water, or new exit.

## CW-PS-19 — Sally: The Door Everyone Watched
**Previous:** CW-PS-09, A Seat by the Door. **Route:** Mended Drum public frontage → Market approach → Carrot.

Three people watch a doorway as though they expect someone to leave. Sally suspects an exit signal until she sees the same motion on a quiet pass. They're waiting to collect pay that another person has been claiming on their behalf.

**What carries over:** If you stopped the first exchange immediately, a worker approaches because they saw the Watch intervene. If you kept talking to trace the collector, the person who paid on the previous night now identifies the token used for this one.

**What you do together:** Ask Sally to keep the pay attendant in conversation so you can watch who receives the token. Or compare the workers' own accounts privately. Choose between helping them recover wages now and witnessing enough to identify the false claimant. Sally's debrief includes what made her revise her first reading of the room.

**Release check:** Watching the door is never proof by itself. Dialogue and visual evidence work without audio or a new NPC capability.

## CW-PS-20 — Carrot: The Agreement Nobody Signed
**Previous:** CW-PS-10, Three People, One Key. **Route:** Watch Square → public yard frontage → Market → Watch Square.

The three yard users agree on access, but a fourth person turns up just before the shared slot ends, insisting they hold the final say. The agreement was real. A written copy circulating in town adds a line that none of the original parties accepted.

**What carries over:** If you settled access without proving fraud, the previous parties start by defending their agreement together. If you retained a witness for the fraud report, that person brings both versions of the copy. A filed partial report never rewards the person making the new claim.

**What you do together:** Ask Carrot to keep the parties talking long enough for you to compare the wording. Choose a witnessed reaffirmation to protect access immediately, or retain the evidence and trace who distributed the altered copy. The Watch helps people keep their own agreement; it doesn't issue them new rights.

**Release check:** No permanent yard permissions, property rule or door state changes. Keep both the original report and the new outcome independently visible.

## Pilot: two partners, two consequences

Build Nobby's and Colon's first missions before their follow-ups. For the follow-up proof, use four saved fixtures:

| Earlier choice | What the returning player sees first | What still needs proving |
|---|---|---|
| Nobby: publicly clear the child | The child describes the short-change patter | Who kept the missing change |
| Nobby: keep watching the lookout | A customer recognises the lookout | Who received the change, and when |
| Colon: disperse the crowd | The porter asks for another safe queue | Whether any written instruction was deliberately altered |
| Colon: hold a safe line and investigate the load | A supplier brings the disputed slip | Who issued each instruction |

Keep each follow-up to two meaningful investigative stops plus the debrief in the first release. Reuse the existing report snapshot or a case-local record as appropriate. Persist only the specific facts needed to select the return lead; use existing mentor identity and eligibility. No global relationship score.

Verify both earlier-outcome branches for each pilot, save/resume on each at the first lead and before filing, one reward per instance, neutral opening for a compatible older save, no stale partner on reload, and no changes to ordinary Duty or the opening patrol. Verify exact legal standing points on the live integration candidate; keep phone and hosted results separate.
